# Measurement and Recovery Protocol

## 1. Datum

Before the scored attempt, establish a fixed local ground reference.

Measure the payload reference point at rest:

```text
h_0
```

The same payload reference point is used throughout the attempt.

## 2. Height

The scored height is vertical displacement, not path length:

```text
H = h_max - h_0
```

Horizontal drift does not increase the score.

## 3. Verification hold

The payload must remain at or above the claimed qualifying height for at least **5 continuous seconds**.

The measurement system should have sufficient accuracy that the reported score is not dominated by instrument uncertainty.

Raw measurement evidence should be retained.

## 4. Accepted measurement methods

The exact instrument may vary with scale, but the method must be independently auditable.

Examples include:

- calibrated tape/line measurement for short lifts;
- laser range finding;
- surveyed reference markers;
- photogrammetry against calibrated geometry;
- instrumented tether length plus independently verified geometry;
- altitude sensing with an independently checked baseline for appropriate outdoor tests.

The method and uncertainty must be reported.

## 5. Recovery

The mission is not complete at maximum height.

The complete payload must return intentionally and safely to ground level.

Uncontrolled falling or uncontrolled release is not a valid recovery method.

## 6. Recovery area

Each venue must define a recovery area **before** the scored run.

Its size is recorded with the result and may depend on the physical test environment.

The recovery area must not be changed after ascent begins.

## 7. Airborne systems

Airborne or buoyant systems are allowed only where lawful and safe.

They must include a credible method to prevent uncontrolled loss of the payload or equipment.

For tethered systems, the tether and reel mechanism are part of the lifting system and therefore normally count against the physical budget.

## 8. Failure conditions

The run is not an official scored success if:

- payload is lost;
- payload is damaged beyond the allowed benchmark condition;
- recovery requires human physical assistance;
- emergency intervention occurs;
- claimed height cannot be independently verified;
- the verification hold is not achieved;
- the payload lands outside the preregistered recovery area;
- the system violates the benchmark's safety or legal constraints.

The maximum observed height should still be retained as diagnostic data.
